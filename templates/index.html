Problem Definition & Academic Scope

In modern university campuses and collaborative research networks, students and early-career researchers struggle with informational silos and fragmented peer discovery. Traditional recommendation systems rely purely on naive demographic filters or spam-heavy 1-hop friend counts.

This project formulates social discovery as an AI Heuristic Search and Knowledge Representation problem. By modeling relationships as a weighted multi-relational graph, we synthesize:

  • Minimum Spanning Tree: Extracts the core backbone of highest-affinity connections.
  • Directed Acyclic Graphs (DAG): Resolves skill dependencies into structured mentorship tiers.
  • Hybrid AI Link Prediction: Blends topological proximity (Adamic-Adar) with multi-hot semantic cosine similarity and A* social intro pathfinding.

Industrial Relevance & Case Studies

LinkedIn ("People You May Know - PYMK"): Uses Adamic-Adar index and triadic closure to prioritize shared connections with small in-degrees over hyper-connected celebrity accounts.
Meta / Facebook Graph Search: Uses Kruskal-style backbone extraction to optimize distributed data replication across global server clusters, minimizing cross-datacenter latency.
ResearchGate & Academic Collaboration: Uses topological sorting to order prerequisite coursework and hierarchical peer-review mentorship ladders.
